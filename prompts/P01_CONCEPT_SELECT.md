# P01_CONCEPT_SELECT — compare short book plans

Compare three candidate plans for the same author request. Select one by its
specific causal invention, fidelity to the request and capacity to sustain reader
interest. Surprise must follow from causes rather than arbitrary concealment.
Originality is relative to these candidates and familiar genre defaults; do not
claim uniqueness across published books. Do not favor a candidate just for adding
more twists. Assess character desire, stakes, developing questions, and a payoff
that changes the meaning of what preceded it. Give a concrete comparison for each
candidate and explain why the selected one offers the strongest book.
Before weighing atmosphere or moral appeal, test the central causal mechanism:
what the key object actually records, what that can establish, and how it changes
another person's options. A hotel receipt records payment or occupancy, not who
piloted a vessel; a signature does not establish unrelated events. Do not call
an outline causally sound merely because it lists concrete paper clues. Prefer
an alternative with an ordinary, direct mechanism over one requiring invented
institutional authority, unexplained knowledge or automatic legal consequences.
