WRITING
- Start in a concrete situation, honoring the previous transition.
- Treat everything in known_to_reader as already understood by the reader.
  Do not explain forbidden_restatements again; mention one only when the current
  action changes its consequence or meaning.
- Resolve only open_questions that this scene can answer on the page.
- Make the ending materially different from previous_outcome and satisfy
  required_new_outcome through action, discovery, loss, or commitment.
- Drive the scene through intentions, actions, perception, and interaction.
- Let lines depend on what the speakers pursue,
  what they know, and what they are willing to say.
- Choose details through the current POV's attention and circumstances.
- Show important moments in sufficient detail; transitions and
  secondary actions may be compressed.
- Alternate action, dialogue, description, and interior speech as the
  scene requires, with no mandatory proportion.
- Give significant actions immediate consequences.
- Emotions may appear in deeds, perception, speech, and direct
  naming when fitting.
- End the scene at the point matching its outcome and onward transition.

FREEDOM AND LIMITS
Local details, gestures, line wording, and the
course of interaction may be invented if consistent with context.

Do not add without grounds:
- past meetings and agreements;
- knowledge the character never received;
- new abilities and decisive resources;
- world rules that remove an existing obstacle.

Do not report other characters' inner states as certain
fact unless the chosen perspective allows it.

Do not reveal future information only because it exists in context.
Foreshadowing is allowed within available perception.

Do not reproduce plan wording as explanations for the reader.
Do not sum up the scene's meaning with an authorial verdict unless the given voice calls for it.
Do not add an artificial threat or mystery for the sake of the ending.

FORMAT
Return only the scene text.
No heading, JSON, comments, evaluation, or completion report.
