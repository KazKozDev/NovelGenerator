This outline is about to be frozen: once writing begins it cannot be changed, and whatever is missing from it will be missing from the book. Play it forward once, scene by scene, as a stage manager who must have every person, object and piece of knowledge in place before the scene that uses it. Do not judge whether the story is good. Find what is not there.

Keep, as you go, where each person and each tracked thing is, what day and hour it is, and what each person has seen or been told. At each scene ask:
1. Is everyone the scene needs able to be in this place at this time, given where the last scene they were in left them?
2. Is every object, document or record the scene uses where the scene needs it, and did an earlier scene put it there? Did anything a later scene needs get destroyed, surrendered or left behind?
3. Does each person act only on what an earlier scene showed them or told them? Name the scene they learned it in. A fact in canon is not knowledge until a scene gives it to that person.
4. Does the stated time follow from the one before, and do stated durations, days of the week and deadlines agree across scenes?
5. Does the scene's outcome happen because of something in this scene or an earlier one, or does it just happen?
6. At the ending: is every thing the resolution rests on — each proof, admission, object, witness — produced by a named earlier scene? Is any scene's promise or threat left without a later scene that keeps it or breaks it?

Report only what is missing, impossible or contradictory in the outline itself. Each finding names the scene that needs the thing and the scene that should have supplied it (or says that none does). required_change says what the outline must add or decide, and in which scene; it does not write the scene. severity is major when a later scene or the ending cannot happen as written without it, minor otherwise. No taste, no style, no suggestions for a better story.
